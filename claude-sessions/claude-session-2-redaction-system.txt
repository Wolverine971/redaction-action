⏺ This is greenfield: the repo has only the database setup and a health check. You described most of the system already, so here's how I'd build it, including the better option for the two-pass question, followed by four decisions for you.
  
  **How I'd build it**
  
  - **The AI identifies the people; plain search finds every mention.** The model's job is to find "John Smith," his variants ("John," "J. Smith," "Mr. Smith") and each address. Then ordinary string search sweeps the whole document for every variant. A model can miss a mention on page 87, but a search won't, and that's what makes "redact every John" reliable.
  - **Locations are page → paragraph → exact characters.** Knowing the paragraph isn't enough to black out the right words. The model never reports positions; our code finds them, so highlights always land exactly.
  - **SSNs come from pattern matching, not the AI.** That covers full numbers with or without dashes, masked forms like `XXX-XX-1234`, and last-four mentions like "ending in 1234."
  - **John vs. Steve Smith only needs one pass.** The review list is grouped by person, not by matching text. Each person has their own list of names. A mention that fits two people ("Mr. Smith") goes to a short "Who is this?" queue: the AI guesses from the surrounding sentence and you confirm with one click. Approving John redacts only John's mentions. Anything still unresolved at export gets redacted, because over-redacting costs less than leaking.
  
  **Defaults unless you object:** Claude does the detection. There's no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` in `.env.local` yet, so you'll need to add one. Company names appear as their own group but are kept by default. All documents in one batch share one list of people, so a decision about John applies everywhere. Reviewers can also select text to add anything the AI missed.
  
  **Lean vs. ambitious:**
  - **Lean** is your flow: upload → detect → a list grouped by person with mention counts → click a mention to jump to it → approve or reject → export.
  - **Ambitious** adds a test set of made-up documents with planted names and SSNs (including a Smith-family trap), plus a score showing what percentage we catch. A measured catch rate makes a take-home stand out, and "I measured how many we caught instead of eyeballing it" is a strong story for your FDE interviews.

⏺ I fixed the estimate. On the sample it now starts at about 11 minutes and only counts down. I confirmed this on two real runs, which took 12.5 and 12 minutes. Before, it swung 11 → 9 → 22 → 3.
  
  **What was wrong:**
  - **Wrong math for parallel sections.** The sample is split into 11 sections and the model reads 4 at a time. The countdown assumed one at a time, so when the first section finished it predicted roughly 4× too long.
  - **It climbed while waiting.** Between finished sections the estimate went up every second, then dropped sharply when the next batch finished. That was the 22 → 3 swing.
  - **The starting number was too high.** The 11 minutes you saw first came from a cruder formula.
  
  **What it does now:**
  - **One shared plan.** The number on the Find redactions button and the live countdown come from the same plan, built from the exact sections the scanner will run.
  - **The live countdown refines that plan** from how fast sections actually finish. It eases toward each new estimate instead of jumping, and the progress bar never moves backward.
  - **Honest waiting message when the model stalls.** The free model sometimes hangs on one section: one took 5 minutes in the first run, and another took 9 in the second. The screen now says "The last section is taking longer than usual (6 min) · then ~2 min" instead of sitting on "45 sec" for minutes.
  - **It learns from each finished batch.** That's what moved the sample's estimate up to 11 minutes after the first slow run.
  
  **Accuracy limits:**
  - **A hang can't be predicted until it happens.** The free model's speed swings a lot: the same sample ran in 8.4 and 12.5 minutes. Before a section goes long, the countdown can read a few minutes low; once it does, the waiting message takes over.
  - **Small batches may read long for now.** A 3-document batch may show about 4 minutes where it takes about 2.5, because one slow run is in the learned average. This evens out as more batches finish, and it errs long rather than short.
  
  Typecheck, lint and all 70 tests pass. Both real runs are now regression tests, and `pnpm estimate:replay` prints how the countdown handles slow, fast and stalled models. Nothing is committed.
  
  **Two options to speed up the runs themselves (not built):**
  - **Cheap:** when a section runs 2× longer than usual, send a second copy and keep whichever finishes first. That would likely have cut 3–5 minutes from both runs.
  - **Bigger:** move to a paid model. Speed would be steadier and estimates tighter, and that's also the zero-data-retention route we discussed for real client documents.
